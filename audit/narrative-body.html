01 / The AI stack

Intelligence has
a supply chain.

Start with the whole system. Then work from electricity to the customer, one horizontal layer at a time.

AI is not one market with one margin structure. It is a stack of businesses selling different things: power, processors, capacity, intelligence and useful products. A dollar of application revenue can also appear in a model provider's revenue and a cloud provider's revenue. Adding those revenues does not reveal the size of the end market.

The better question is where each layer creates value, what it must buy from the layer below, and what remains after it pays the bill. Technology leaders can span several layers. That does not make the layers economically interchangeable.

Energy / the delivery constraint

A power announcement is not a connected data center.

AI consumes electricity in particular places at particular times. A national generation surplus does not supply a campus that lacks an interconnection, transformers or permits. The constraint is executable delivery, not merely a global energy total.

The IEA's updated outlook puts global data-center electricity consumption at {{value:energy2025}} in 2025 and projects {{value:energy2030}} in 2030. These figures cover all data centers, not only AI. They establish the scale of the infrastructure problem, not the return on any individual power project. {{cite:energy2025}}

Silicon / the architecture constraint

More competition does not make accelerators interchangeable.

General-purpose accelerators compete with hyperscaler-designed chips and specialized inference architectures. The trade-off is not just price per chip. It includes memory, networking, software compatibility, utilization, supply and the workloads an architecture can actually serve.

NVIDIA's {{value:nvidiaQuarter}} of Q2 FY2027 revenue is a reported quarter, not an annual market estimate. Etched's first rack shipment offers a different kind of evidence: progress toward a specialized inference product. Revenue, a shipment milestone and a financing announcement should not occupy the same performance leaderboard. {{cite:nvidiaQuarter}} {{cite:etchedRound}}

Models / the capability and control constraint

Buy capability. Choose control. Measure completed work.

The model layer is not one race with one winner. Buyers choose among frontier capability, latency, price, modality, deployment control and policy constraints. A model that wins a benchmark may not win the customer's workload after tools, retries, human review and integration are included.

Standard API prices illustrate a barbell: {{value:priceSmall}} at the small-model end, {{value:priceWorkhorse}} for workhorse models and {{value:pricePremium}} for premium models, each per million input/output tokens. This is a list-price comparison, not the cost of equivalent successful work. Context thresholds, caching and temporary launch pricing matter. {{cite:priceSmall}} {{cite:priceClaude}}

Chinese open-weight models belong in the core competitive picture. DeepSeek's MIT-licensed V4.1 Flash is a concrete hosting-control option. Kimi and GLM broaden the alternatives, but their exact releases and licenses need to be checked individually. Downloadable weights are not the same promise as unrestricted use, cheap serving or frontier parity. {{cite:deepseek}} {{cite:kimi}} {{cite:glm}}

What a benchmark cannot settle

Compare the same tasks, success threshold, harness and cost basis. Hold out production-like tasks, rerun them, and record error severity as well as success. The surrounding context and tools can materially change an agent's results without changing its model weights. {{cite:aiResearch}}

Specialized video, image, voice and world models have different delivery constraints. Evaluate controllability, rights, latency and cost per useful output rather than treating parameter counts or a modality leaderboard as a business ranking.

Thinking Machines Lab, Safe Superintelligence, AMI Labs, humans&, Ineffable Intelligence, Reflection AI, Goodfire and World Labs pursue different architectures and commercial strategies. A financing round is evidence of investor appetite, not a common measure of their technical progress or product demand. Compare released products, reproducible results, customer use and the cost of the next milestone. {{cite:aiResearch}}

Applications and distribution / the customer constraint

The customer buys a result, not a model ranking.

Applications package intelligence into a workflow, an interface and a relationship. The model vendor can also own that interface, so independent application companies need more than access to the same API: context, integration, a route to the customer, or a reliably better result.

Demand is real, but its measures are not interchangeable. Anthropic's {{value:anthropicRevenue}} is reported Q3 revenue. OpenAI's {{value:openaiRevenue}} is an annualized September pace with a different partner-revenue treatment. Higgsfield's {{value:higgsfieldRevenue}} is a company-disclosed four-week pace multiplied by 13. None is a clean, additive application-market total. {{cite:anthropicRevenue}} {{cite:openaiRevenue}} {{cite:higgsfieldRevenue}}

02 / Agentic revolution and stack

From an answer
to an action.

An agent is a working system, not just a more capable model.

A model can propose a refund. An agent can look up the order, check policy, prepare the transaction, seek approval where required, execute it and verify the result. The difference is a loop with tools, state and consequences, not a different chat window.

This shifts the engineering problem from generating plausible output to finishing authorized work. A useful agent must know what it can access, what counts as success, when to retry, and when a human must take over. More autonomy makes the boundaries more important, not less.

Understand intentPlanLoad contextAct through toolsVerifyRecover or escalate
Across the loopIdentity · permissions · isolation · evaluations · audit trail · human approval

The agentic stack is a different map.

The economic stack explains who supplies whom. The agentic stack explains how a product operates. User interfaces, orchestration, memory, tool access, models, observability and security are software responsibilities, not seven new independent revenue pools.

The architecture is worth understanding because failures often live between components. The right model with the wrong permissions can do damage; a good plan with incomplete context can fail quietly; a successful tool call can still produce the wrong business result.

03 / Business Models

What are you selling?
What do you keep?

Separate the pricing unit, the delivery model and the financing model.

A subscription, a usage meter and an outcome fee answer how the customer pays. They do not tell us who performs the service, who owns the relationship, or who is liable when the work fails. Those questions determine the business underneath the invoice.

AI also makes cost of delivery visible again. Inference, tools, human review, customer support and exceptions can grow with usage. A software interface does not guarantee traditional SaaS margins, and outcome pricing moves more execution risk onto the vendor. Bessemer's pricing research explicitly warns against one-size-fits-all benchmarks. {{cite:pricingFramework}}

Access

Subscription / seat

Predictable budget. Usage and delivery cost can vary underneath a fixed fee.

Consumption

Usage / transaction

Charges follow activity. The buyer must still connect the meter to business value.

Completed work

Workflow / outcome

A clearer value unit. The vendor absorbs ambiguity, retries and failure cost.

Hybrid pricing is not an intermediate failure on the way to a universal outcome model. A platform fee can cover availability and integration, while usage or outcome charges capture expansion. The right design depends on what the customer values and what the vendor can measure and control. {{cite:pricingFramework}}

The inference tax is a company-level question.

Cheaper tokens can increase margin, finance more capable execution, or disappear into price competition. Customers can receive more value while the supplier retains less. Underwrite cost per successful unit of work and retained cash, not the API list price in isolation.

Compare cohorts using the same cost classifications. A service operator can include labor or pass-through purchases in revenue and COGS; a software vendor may report a narrower net revenue base. A lower reported gross margin is not, by itself, evidence of worse automation. Conversely, removing required human work from COGS does not make it disappear. {{cite:pricingFramework}} {{cite:serviceFramework}}

A rollup does not inherently have lower margins than an AI SaaS company. Its inherited service mix, pass-throughs, integration expenses, automation, acquisition price and debt determine the result. EBITDA excludes interest and capital spending, so it cannot stand in for cash available to fund the next acquisition.

We do not assign fixed gross-margin or EBITDA bands to the three strategies. Even a rapidly growing AI tool can have thin or negative delivery economics; a revenue multiple without a credible gross-profit and retention denominator is incomplete.

Other pricing mechanisms

Advertising, take rates, shared savings and service fees can coexist with subscriptions. Evaluate monetizable demand, attribution, user trust and delivery cost before asserting that one mechanism subsidizes another.

04 / Services

Sell the tool.
Sell the work.
Buy the business.

Three attack angles, defined by who owns the customer and delivery.

“AI for services” describes several fundamentally different companies. One sells software to a professional. Another becomes the provider. A third acquires providers and changes how they operate. The underlying AI can be similar while distribution, responsibility, financing and the path to cash are completely different.

Sequoia's copilot/autopilot distinction asks whether the company sells the tool or the work. Bessemer's AI-native services framework asks whether automation reaches delivery and whether the company retains the surplus. Our third angle makes acquisition-led distribution explicit rather than hiding it inside the operator category. {{cite:sequoiaServices}} {{cite:serviceFramework}}

Market structure is a fit test, not a law.

Software can work well when enough providers have similar workflows and budgets to support repeatable sales. A new operator can win where the customer will switch for better price, speed or quality, whether supply is concentrated or fragmented. A rollup can make sense when relationships are sticky and acquisition plus integration is a better distribution route than winning accounts from scratch.

Fragmentation alone is insufficient. The work must be automatable, the acquired customers must stay, and cash conversion must survive integration and debt service. More cash flow can support more debt only when lenders accept its durability. It is not a perpetual acquisition machine.

Compare the same market through three entry routesExamples, DVC companies and the failure mode of each approach+

Software to incumbents

Harvey sells legal software to law firms and in-house teams; it is not a law firm. FleetWorks sells freight workflow software; Solve Intelligence sells tools for patent work. These are software suppliers, not automatically outsourced providers. {{cite:harveyRound}}

Failure mode: low willingness to pay, costly support, customer consolidation, or model vendors absorbing the workflow.

Vertical agentic operator

Crosby sells legal work; WithCoverage delivers insurance brokerage; Strala is an AI-native claims administrator. These are service-delivery examples, not evidence that all underlying work is autonomous. {{cite:sequoiaServices}} {{cite:serviceFramework}}

Failure mode: acquisition costs, licensing, exceptions or liability consume the automation surplus.

Agentic rollup

Dwelly and Fura are DVC rollup examples. Fura uses FleetWorks: the acquired operator and the software supplier can both participate in the same value chain. There is no requirement that one strategy eliminate the others.

Failure mode: overpaying, losing customers or key staff, failing to integrate operations, or financing temporary savings with durable debt.

05 / Healthcare

Efficiency for whom?
Savings for whom?

Trace the payer, the provider, the workflow and the patient before calling an improvement a system-wide saving.

The US healthcare baseline is {{value:healthSpending}} of 2024 spending, the latest historical year in the CMS fact sheet. It is not a live 2026 market estimate. The payment system matters because an AI product can reduce administrative work, increase provider revenue, improve care or reduce total spending. Those are different outcomes. {{cite:healthSpending}}

The latest Bessemer/Bain scorecard reports {{value:healthRoi}} average realized ROI among surveyed healthcare executives. Provider revenue-cycle respondents reported much greater agent autonomy than clinical respondents. These are buyer-reported results, not randomized evidence that clinical autonomy is ready or that AI lowers national spending. {{cite:healthRoi}} {{cite:healthRcmAutonomy}} {{cite:healthClinicalAutonomy}}

{{metric:healthRoi}}{{metric:healthRcmAutonomy}}{{metric:healthClinicalAutonomy}}

A more efficient provider can submit a larger bill.

Under fee-for-service, better documentation, coding and collections can raise captured revenue without reducing treatment cost or spending. PwC reports that {{value:healthInflator}} of surveyed health plans rank AI-enabled provider revenue capture among their top three cost inflators. That is a payer expectation and a mechanism to investigate, not a measured estimate of AI's causal effect on inflation. {{cite:healthInflator}}

Clinical AI faces another set of gates: trust, accountable decision-making, reimbursement and liability. The investor must identify the buyer and the cash recipient, then distinguish support for a clinician from responsibility for an autonomous clinical decision.

    07 / Investing and valuations

    Value created
    is not value captured.

    AI can be transformative while individual investments disappoint.

    The report's central investment question is not whether AI is useful. It is which businesses retain durable profit as capability becomes cheaper and more widely available. Customers can receive an enormous surplus while suppliers compete away the margin.

    That is why the underwriting sequence matters. Establish useful capability, customer willingness to pay, delivery economics and defensibility before applying a valuation. A financing round tells us what a particular transaction priced. It does not settle the other questions.

    CapabilityCustomer valueCaptured profit and cashDurabilityReturn at the entry price

    Use the denominator that matches the business.

    Application companies need retention and gross profit after inference and review. Service operators need profitable delivery and controlled liability. Rollups need organic improvement and cash after integration and debt service. Infrastructure needs contracted demand, utilization and returns on capital. Robotics needs deployment economics, not a software multiple attached to a hardware story.

    Cursor's thin delivery margins are a reason to question comparisons with high-margin SaaS, not automatically evidence of an irrational multiple. More generally, reported revenue, annualized pace, contracted ARR, gross profit and free cash flow cannot substitute for one another. We do not derive fresh multiples from mismatched dates or incomparable revenue policies.

    Funding concentration remains material: Crunchbase reports {{value:aiFundingShare}} of Q3 global venture funding went to AI-driven companies. That is a capital-allocation observation, not proof that every layer is overvalued or that the resulting capacity will earn its cost of capital. {{cite:aiFundingShare}}

    Open the underwriting questionsThe variables that can change the investment conclusion+

    What survives cheaper intelligence?

    Distribution, embedded context, workflow integration, rights, trust, regulatory responsibility or a scarce physical input. Model access alone is not a durable answer.

    Who keeps the saving?

    The customer, the software vendor, the operator, labor, the cloud provider or the lender. Track pricing and cash conversion, not just efficiency.

    What must be financed?

    Training, inference, onboarding, acquisitions, working capital or equipment. Equity and debt can fund several models; neither is a business-model category.

    Disconfirming evidence belongs inside diligence: falling retention, worsening task contribution, rising interventions, weak integration or customers capturing all the price improvement. The separate bear-case slide remains deferred.
